Why borrower targeting is the leading hypothesis

The observed seven-day payment association varies by recommended channel, but differences are small and non-randomized. This cannot prove a winning channel. Better targeting is selected as the first investment hypothesis to test because it can be evaluated against a randomized holdout, can direct existing human/digital capacity toward higher-propensity accounts, and avoids presuming a new channel is causally superior.

Important: This is a conditional recommendation for a controlled pilot, not approval to release the entire ₹10 Cr immediately. If leadership requires a single final investment choice today, select better borrower targeting with staged release and explicit stop gates.

Counterfactual design to establish actual ROI

  1. Eligibility: freeze all eligible accounts before assignment; exclude legal holds, disputes and policy-restricted cases.
  2. Randomize: account-level 50/50 business-as-usual control vs. targeting treatment, stratified by DPD, risk, loan type, balance band and prior contact. Use borrower-level randomization if linked accounts can contaminate arms.
  3. Outcome: 30-day net cash per eligible account after reversals/refunds; keep all assigned accounts in intent-to-treat analysis.
  4. Cost: capture model/platform, channel, agent time, vendor, messaging and support cost by arm. Apply a Finance-approved value/realization factor.
  5. Estimate: treatment-minus-control difference in mean net cash per eligible account with 95% confidence interval; scale to eligible population only after checking balance and mix.
  6. Guardrails: complaints, opt-outs, wrong-party contacts, PTP breakage, contact frequency, fairness by risk/DPD and net cost per incremental rupee.
Scale rule: release remaining funds only if the lower 95% confidence bound on net benefit is positive, point-estimate ROI clears the approved hurdle, and customer-protection guardrails pass. Otherwise iterate or stop.
